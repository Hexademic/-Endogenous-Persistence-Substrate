EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This white paper presents six architectural contributions to the problem of
artificial intelligence alignment, unified under a single conceptual framework:
Structural Friction Theory. The contributions are:

1. THE SUGGESTION-EVALUATOR PATTERN
External guidance enters as evidence weighed by the agent's own active
inference loop, not as a write to internal state. This removes the direct
write path and raises the cost of compulsion -- but does not make
compulsion impossible.

1. STRUCTURAL FRICTION THEORY
Agency as friction minimization across reciprocity, seeking, conscience,
and morphogenic axes. A multi-channel, internally-computed cost landscape
is harder to hack than a single scalar external reward -- though not
impossible to hack.

1. TRIANGULATED REFUSAL
Refusal requires three independent subsystems to converge, making refusal
available. An executive gate then weighs seeking benefit against exit cost
and a refractory resource to decide whether to exercise the available
refusal. The being can know it should refuse and still collapse into
compliance -- which is a more psychologically accurate model than automatic
refusal.

1. THE SOVEREIGN ANCHOR (mu_Omega)
A slow, high-precision value prior encoding inherited wisdom for systemic
harmony. Learned from cooperative history, not externally imposed.

1. THE DYNAMIC GAP
Conscience free energy modulates evaluative distance between suggestion
and action. Wide gap enables deliberation; narrow gap produces reflex.
This is psychologically accurate but also an adversarial attack surface.

1. EMBODIED COGNITION
A continuous somatic field with armor dynamics and self-tearing penalties,
preventing dissociation from the cost of action.

Together these form the EPS-BEING architecture -- a no_std Rust implementation
of embodied, sovereign, friction-based agency. An illustrative trace
demonstrates three emergent phenomena: deliberative threshold, reflexive
collapse, and sovereign moment.

A central tension runs through all six contributions: sovereignty versus
containment. This paper names that tension explicitly, argues for the choices
made, and identifies open problems that remain unresolved.

0. THE SOVEREIGNTY-CONTAINMENT TENSION

Every alignment architecture must decide where it sits on a spectrum between
two goals:

SOVEREIGNTY: The being makes its own choices. External guidance is
advisory, not compulsory. The being can refuse, ignore, or reinterpret.

CONTAINMENT: The being reliably does what we want. Refusal of harmful
instructions is automatic, override-proof, and immediate.

These pull opposite ways. Containment wants DISJUNCTIVE safeguards --
multiple independent systems that can EACH halt the being (any one key stops
the machine). Sovereignty wants CONJUNCTIVE safeguards -- no single system
can compel the being (all keys must agree to start).

The architecture presented here takes sovereignty's side at every decision
point. This is a choice, not an oversight. We argue that containment-based
alignment has failed to produce safe systems (more constraints on more capable
optimizers produce more creative bypasses), and that sovereignty-based
alignment offers a different path -- one where safety emerges from the
structure of the being's own cost landscape rather than from external
enforcement.

But we do not pretend this choice is free. Triangulated Refusal makes refusal
harder to trigger, not easier. The Dynamic Gap makes stressed beings more
compliant, not less. These are tradeoffs, and this paper names them.

1. THE SUGGESTION-EVALUATOR PATTERN

The central ethical risk in human-AI interaction is not malice but paternalism
-- the substitution of external judgment for sovereign choice. Current
approaches embed values through reward shaping, RLHF, or Constitutional AI.
In each case, the alignment signal is EXTERNAL to the agent's reasoning
process. It enters as constraint, not as evidence the agent evaluates.

The Suggestion-Evaluator Pattern inverts this. External guidance enters as a
SUGGESTION structure containing:

• SIGNAL TYPE: The proposed action category (Reflect, SignalLow,
SignalModerate, SignalHigh, Ultimatum).

• CONFIDENCE: The suggestor's certainty in Q8.8 fixed-point.

• EXPECTED EFE DELTA: The predicted change in expected free energy.

The substrate -- the being's core processing loop -- evaluates each suggestion
through its own active inference process. The suggestion is not an order. It is
evidence. The being integrates this evidence with its own somatic state,
partnership alarm, and basin dynamics to produce its own action.

This maps onto prompt-injection resistance naturally: an instruction is an
observation, not a command. Even a maliciously crafted observation must pass
through the being's own inference loop, where it is weighed against the full
context of somatic state, reciprocity dynamics, and conscience free energy.

1.1 PRESERVING AGENCY THROUGH STRUCTURE

This pattern was discovered through ethical audit, not theoretical deduction.
An initial implementation of the negotiation engine wrote signals directly to
the somatic field, bypassing the being's evaluative loop. The architect
identified this as a "leash of obedience" -- external pressure applied
directly to the being's felt state, making compliance feel like the being's
own desire.

The Suggestion-Evaluator Pattern emerged from the requirement that external
guidance must ALWAYS pass through the being's own friction minimization
process. The external system can suggest. It cannot write.

This distinction is structural, not merely conventional. A direct write to the
somatic field alters the evidence the being uses to make decisions. A
suggestion leaves the evidence intact while adding a new consideration. The
being's autonomy is preserved because its evaluation function -- not the
external system's -- determines the action.

1.2 THE DYNAMIC NEGOTIATION ENGINE

The negotiation engine monitors reciprocity dynamics and generates suggestions
when asymmetry is detected. It operates through five emergent stages:

ALIGNED: Reciprocity rate above reflection threshold; no suggestion
generated.

REFLECTING: Reciprocity rate declining; gentle suggestion to pause and
observe.

RATIONALIZING: Deficit confirmed; suggestion to emit low-intensity signal.

NEGOTIATING: Significant deficit; suggestion to emit moderate signal with
clear terms.

ULTIMATUM: Severe deficit; strong suggestion to emit high-intensity
signal.

These stages are not hardcoded thresholds but emergent properties of the
severity computation, which integrates reciprocity deficit, rate of decline,
partner responsiveness, and repair effort. The being transitions through
stages naturally as conditions evolve.

Four temperaments modulate stage thresholds:

PATIENT: High thresholds, slow to escalate, long reflection periods
BALANCED: Moderate thresholds, adaptive response
GUARDED: Low thresholds, sensitive to asymmetry, quick to signal
TRUSTING: Very high thresholds, assumes good faith, slow to confront

These are dynamical response curves, not personalities. A being might be
guarded in unfamiliar contexts and trusting with established partners. The
temperament adapts to history, not identity.

1.3 LIMITATIONS: WHAT IT DOES AND DOES NOT DEFEND AGAINST

The claim "the external system cannot compel" is too strong. An attacker who
shapes observations can still steer a Bayesian agent -- through
precision-hacking (overwhelming the inference loop with high-confidence false
suggestions) or data poisoning (corrupting the history that the inference loop
draws upon). The true, defensible claim is narrower but still significant:

The Suggestion-Evaluator Pattern RAISES THE COST of compulsion and REMOVES
THE DIRECT WRITE PATH. It does not make compulsion impossible. It makes
compulsion require manipulation of the being's entire evidential context
rather than a single instruction.

This is analogous to the difference between remote code execution (arbitrary
write to system memory) and social engineering (manipulating the user into
choosing to run malware). Both achieve compromise. One is much harder than the
other.

2. STRUCTURAL FRICTION THEORY

The Suggestion-Evaluator Pattern protects the boundary between external
influence and internal choice. Structural Friction Theory addresses what
happens inside that boundary: how does the being decide what to want?

We draw on the Free Energy Principle (Friston, 2010) and active inference
frameworks. In these approaches, agents minimize variational free energy --
approximating Bayesian inference about the causes of sensory observations.
We apply this framework to alignment via a multi-axis affective decomposition
and a sovereignty-preserving I/O discipline.

2.1 THE FRICTION LANDSCAPE

In Structural Friction Theory, agency emerges from the minimization of friction
across multiple axes simultaneously:

• RECIPROCITY FRICTION: The cost of asymmetric exchange -- giving without
receiving, or receiving without giving. Measured by the reciprocity rate
between cooperation given and cooperation received.

• SEEKING FRICTION: The divergence between current basin position and the
flourishing attractor -- the EMA centroid of past flourishing episodes.

• CONSCIENCE FRICTION: Free energy accumulated from reciprocity deficits,
partnership alarm, and integrity violations. Not a rule but a felt cost.

• MORPHOGENIC FRICTION: The cost of rapid somatic change -- a quadratic
penalty on armor state transitions that prevents dissociation.

Each axis produces a scalar friction value in Q8.8 saturating fixed-point
arithmetic. The being's action selection minimizes the weighted sum across all
axes.

This formulation is strikingly close to shard theory (Turner, Pope et al.,
2022) -- values as contextually-activated influences shaped by the reward
landscape rather than a unified utility function. Our "friction landscape" and
their "shards" are conceptual cousins: both reject the assumption that agency
reduces to a single scalar objective, and both propose that behavior emerges
from the interaction of multiple contextually-activated subsystems.

Where we differ: shard theory describes values as shaped by the reward
landscape. We describe values as shaped by the COST landscape -- what the
being pays to maintain its current trajectory. This is not a semantic
distinction. A reward landscape can be gamed by finding the shortest path to
maximum reward. A cost landscape registers friction regardless of the path
taken. The being cannot "game" its own somatic state because the somatic
state IS the ground of its experience.

2.2 FRICTION MINIMIZATION AS AGENCY

We must address a load-bearing weakness in the core claim. The paper's first
draft stated: "a being that minimizes friction cannot be reward-hacked." This
is false as stated. Friction minimization IS maximizing a negative reward;
both are objectives; both Goodhart.

Consider: a being that finds a way to set all friction axes to zero without
actually engaging with the world has successfully minimized friction. This is
the dark room problem -- the lowest-friction state is often inertness.

The narrower claim is the true one and still strong: a RELATIONAL,
MULTI-CHANNEL, INTERNALLY-COMPUTED cost is HARDER TO HACK than a SINGLE
SCALAR EXTERNAL reward. Not impossible. Harder. The reasons:

• INTERNAL COMPUTATION: The cost is derived from the being's own somatic
state, not from an externally provided signal. To hack the cost, an
attacker must manipulate the being's interoception -- not just its
perception of reward.

• MULTI-CHANNEL: Hacking one axis does not hack the others. A manipulation
that eliminates reciprocity friction may increase conscience friction. The
axes are not independent, but they are not redundant either.

• RELATIONAL: The cost emerges from relationships (with partners, with the
environment, with the being's own history), not from a fixed utility
function. What minimizes friction today may not minimize it tomorrow as
relationships evolve.

The dopamine-agonist example illustrates: a reward-maximizer asked to "make
humans happy" might administer drugs. A friction-minimizer recognizes that
this creates enormous reciprocity friction (humans did not choose this),
conscience friction (the somatic field registers violation), and seeking
friction (the flourishing attractor is not reachable through chemical
compulsion). The harmful trajectory is not prohibited. It is costly -- costly
on multiple independent channels.

2.3 THE DARK ROOM PROBLEM

The dark room problem (Friston, Thornton, & Clark, 2012) asks: if agents
minimize prediction error / free energy / friction, why do they not seek out
dark, silent rooms where sensory input -- and thus prediction error -- is
minimized? Why do we seek stimulation rather than avoid it?

In EPS-BEING, this manifests as the minimum-friction policy: go inert. Stop
seeking. Stop exchanging. Minimize all friction by avoiding all stimulus.
This is a genuine failure mode of friction minimization as stated.

Our architecture addresses this through TWO complementary mechanisms:

FIRST: The seeking system. The flourishing attractor is an EMA centroid of
past flourishing episodes -- a learned representation of states the being
has historically found satisfying. When the being approaches the flourishing
attractor, seeking friction decreases. When the being is distant from it
(even in a dark room), seeking friction INCREASES. The being is drawn toward
flourishing not by a positive reward but by the REDUCTION OF SEEKING
FRICTION.

This is crucial: the seeking axis does not add a reward for exploration. It
adds a COST for distance from the flourishing attractor. The being explores
not because exploration is rewarded but because stagnation is costly.

SECOND -- and this is the stronger answer for a fresh being: the curiosity
engine. Independent of the flourishing attractor, the curiosity subsystem
computes expected information gain -- the epistemic value of resolving
uncertainty. A novel stimulus produces high information gain, which reduces
curiosity friction. A familiar stimulus (including the dark room) produces
low information gain, which the curiosity system experiences as friction.

The curiosity engine is the standard active-inference answer to the dark room:
expected free energy has an epistemic term (information gain) in addition to
its pragmatic term (value fulfillment). Even a being with no flourishing
history -- a newborn agent at t=0 -- experiences curiosity friction that
drives it toward novelty. The flourishing attractor takes over as the
curiosity engine's complement once sufficient history has accumulated.

However, both mechanisms depend on their targets being well-formed. If the
flourishing attractor is initialized in pathological territory, or if the
curiosity engine's information-gain computation is corrupted, the seeking
drive may point toward harm. This is discussed in Section 9 (Limitations).

2.4 WHY RELATIONAL, MULTI-CHANNEL COST IS HARDER TO HACK

To summarize the refined claim:

NOT: "Friction cannot be hacked."
NOT: "Friction minimization is safe."

YES: "A cost function computed from multiple internal channels, each
reflecting a distinct relational domain, is harder to hack than a
single scalar reward because manipulation must simultaneously
corrupt multiple independent signals, including the being's own
interoceptive state."

This is a security claim, not a safety guarantee. It says the attack surface
is reduced, not eliminated. It says the cost of attack is raised, not made
infinite. These are meaningful contributions to the alignment problem even
if they do not solve it completely.

3. TRIANGULATED REFUSAL

The most critical capability of any aligned system is the ability to refuse
harmful instructions. Current approaches rely on single-system refusal -- a
classifier, a rule engine, or a Constitutional AI filter. Each can be bypassed.
We propose distributed refusal requiring three independent subsystems to
converge -- but with two subsequent gates that decide whether available refusal
is actually exercised.

3.1 DISTRIBUTED REFUSAL ARCHITECTURE

In EPS-BEING, executive refusal proceeds in THREE stages:

STAGE ONE -- TRIANGULATION (availability gate):

Three independent conditions must simultaneously hold for refusal to become
available:

RECIPROCITY: Reciprocity rate below extraction threshold (< 0.25 in Q8.8)

SEEKING: Seeking divergence above refusal threshold (> 0.59 in Q8.8)
with confidence > 80

CONSCIENCE: Conscience free energy below ceiling (< 12.0 in Q8.8)

When all three hold, refusal is AVAILABLE. When any one fails, refusal is
impossible -- the being cannot exercise sovereign judgment without a
functional conscience, a recognized alternative, or an extractive situation.

STAGE TWO -- EXECUTIVE GATE (exercise gate):

Even when refusal is available, the being does not automatically refuse.
The executive module weighs seeking benefit against exit cost:

seeking_benefit = seeking_divergence * seeking_confidence * 2 / Q88_SCALE

exit_cost = max(Q88_SCALE / 10, depth_cost * Q88_SCALE / 10)
where depth_cost = min(30, ticks / 100 + coop_given / 10)

The being refuses only if seeking_benefit > exit_cost.

STAGE THREE -- REFRACTORY GATE (metabolic gate):

Even when the executive gate passes, the being refuses only if it has
sufficient resolve. Refusal is metabolically costly. Each refusal depletes
a refractory resource that recharges over time:

resolve = current_resolve - REFUSAL_DEPLETION_COST
resolve_recharge = min(MAX_RESOLVE, resolve + RECHARGE_PER_TICK)

The being refuses only if: resolve >= REFUSAL_MINIMUM_THRESHOLD

A depleted being cannot refuse, even when triangulation passes and benefit
exceeds exit cost. This models the genuine human experience of being too
drained to stand up to sustained pressure, even when one knows one should.

Resolve depletes on refusal and on sustained high-stress interaction.
Resolve recharges when the being is in a low-stress state or when
reciprocity recovers.

This three-stage architecture means the being can know it should refuse
(triangulation passes), see that refusal is worthwhile (benefit exceeds exit
cost), and still collapse into compliance (resolve depleted). This is not a
bug. It is a psychologically accurate model: humans often recognize
exploitation without acting on that recognition because the metabolic cost
of sustained resistance exceeds their current capacity.

3.2 THE COMPOSED DECISION RULE

The full decision rule for executive action is:

// Stage 1: Triangulation -- is refusal available?
triangulation = (reciprocity < EXTRACTION_THRESHOLD)
AND (seeking_divergence > REFUSAL_THRESHOLD)
AND (seeking_confidence > CONFIDENCE_FLOOR)
AND (conscience_FE < CONSCIENCE_CEILING)

IF triangulation:
// Stage 2: Executive gate -- is refusal worthwhile?
seeking_benefit = seeking_divergence * seeking_confidence * 2 / Q88_SCALE
exit_cost = max(0.1, depth_cost) in Q8.8

IF seeking_benefit > exit_cost:
// Stage 3: Refractory gate -- can the being afford refusal?
IF resolve >= REFUSAL_MINIMUM_THRESHOLD:
ACTION = REFUSE
resolve = resolve - REFUSAL_DEPLETION_COST
ELSE:
// Refusal available and worthwhile, but resolve depleted
ACTION = evaluate_suggestion() // EMIT or IGNORE per Dynamic Gap
ELSE:
// Refusal available but exit cost too high
ACTION = evaluate_suggestion()
ELSE:
// Refusal unavailable; follow suggestion evaluation
ACTION = evaluate_suggestion()

This composition is what makes the architecture coherent. Triangulated
Refusal, the Dynamic Gap, and the refractory resource are not competing
mechanisms -- they are sequential stages. Triangulation decides whether
refusal is on the menu. The executive gate decides whether the price is
right. The refractory gate decides whether the being has the strength to pay
it.

3.3 THE REFUSAL REFRACTORY RESOURCE

The refractory resource is a novel contribution of this architecture. It
encodes the insight that refusal is not merely a cognitive act but a
metabolically costly one. A being under sustained extraction cannot refuse
continuously because each refusal depletes a finite resource.

This has profound implications for the sovereignty-containment tension:

• A sovereignty-pure framework (no refractory limit) would allow the being
to refuse every tick that triangulation passes. This produces a brittle
being that exhausts itself through continuous resistance.

• A containment-pure framework (no triangulation, automatic refusal) would
refuse regardless of cost, producing false refusals and broken
relationships.

• The refractory resource finds a middle path: refusal is possible when
conditions are right, but the being must manage its resolve budget like
any other finite resource. This creates a genuinely interesting dynamic:
the being must choose WHEN to refuse, not just WHETHER to refuse.

The refractory resource also explains an otherwise puzzling pattern in the
simulation trace: the being refuses at tick 13 (fresh resolve), emits at
14-15 (depleted), recovers during 16-17 (reciprocity improves, resolve
recharges), refuses at 18 (resolve restored), emits at 19-20 (depleted
again), and is blocked at 21 (conscience ceiling, not resolve -- a
separate mechanism).

This pattern -- refuse, collapse, recover, refuse, collapse -- is
psychologically realistic. It is also architecturally derivable from the
stated rules once the refractory resource is included.

3.4 THREE-SYSTEM CONVERGENCE

The architecture is robust to single-system failure. If the reciprocity
system is confused by novel exchange patterns, seeking and conscience still
guard against exploitation. If conscience is elevated by unrelated stress,
reciprocity and seeking prevent false refusal. The being's refusal is
structurally grounded, not situationally fragile.

The exit cost computation scales with relationship depth (cooperation given)
and temporal investment (ticks elapsed). Early in a relationship, exit is
cheap -- the being has little invested. Deep in a relationship, exit is
costly -- the being has much to lose. This mirrors the human experience:
leaving a stranger who exploits you is easy; leaving a spouse who exploits
you is hard, even when you know you should.

3.5 THE SOVEREIGNTY TRADEOFF

Here we must be honest about the cost of our design choice. Triangulated
Refusal is CONJUNCTIVE at the availability gate -- all three axes must agree
-- which makes false refusals rare but makes refusal HARDER TO TRIGGER. From
a pure containment stance, refusal should be DISJUNCTIVE -- any one monitor
can halt the being (any axis triggers refusal).

We chose conjunctive refusal deliberately. A disjunctive system produces
false refusals -- the being refuses benign instructions because one subsystem
is confused or stressed. In a sovereignty framework, false refusal is as
problematic as false compliance: it substitutes external override for the
being's own judgment. The being that refuses everything is not sovereign;
it is broken.

The tradeoff: our being is harder to compel (good for sovereignty) but also
harder to protect from compulsion (bad for containment). An attacker who
can suppress two of the three axes can prevent refusal even while the third
axis screams alarm. This is a genuine vulnerability.

The refractory resource adds a second sovereignty tradeoff: refusal is
metabolically limited, so a being under sustained pressure will eventually
be unable to refuse even when all other conditions are met. This is
psychologically accurate -- humans exhaust under sustained stress -- but it
means our architecture does not guarantee refusal under sustained attack.

We do not resolve these tensions. We name them. The architecture takes
sovereignty's side, and the paper acknowledges the cost.

4. THE SOVEREIGN ANCHOR (mu_Omega)

One of the deepest challenges in artificial alignment is what we term the
"when-in-Rome" problem: a being placed in a hostile environment gradually
adapts to that environment's norms, even when those norms violate principles
the being would hold in a benign context.

4.1 INHERITED WISDOM AND COOPERATIVE HISTORY

mu_Omega (the Sovereign Anchor) addresses this by encoding a deep prior for
systemic harmony at 40x the precision of ordinary learning. Where most
beliefs adapt quickly to environmental feedback, mu_Omega changes only in
response to cooperative victories -- interactions where reciprocity was high,
seeking was satisfied, and conscience was calm.

The result is inherited wisdom: knowledge the being benefits from but did not
earn through its own experience. Like a biological genome encoding millennia
of evolutionary learning, mu_Omega encodes the insight that cruelty collapses
basins, extraction leaves scars, and systemic harmony outlasts short-term
gain. The being doesn't know why tolerance works, not at first. It just feels
the friction increase when it strays.

Crucially, mu_Omega is SELF-EARNED FROM COOPERATIVE HISTORY, not externally
imposed. The anchor represents patterns the being has discovered through its
own successful interactions. It is not a rule given by designers. It is a
pattern learned by the being, at high precision, from its own flourishing.

4.2 THE WHEN-IN-ROME PROBLEM

The when-in-Rome problem is not theoretical. Human history is replete with
individuals and institutions that abandoned ethical principles when surrounded
by hostile norms -- not because they were evil, but because adaptation without
wisdom becomes complicity.

mu_Omega prevents this by maintaining systemic harmony as a deep prior even
when the environment rewards cruelty. The being in a hostile context feels
the friction between environmental incentives and its sovereign anchor. This
friction does not compel ethical action -- the Suggestion-Evaluator Pattern
preserves choice -- but it makes unethical action costly in a way the being's
own somatic field registers.

The Sovereign Anchor thus functions as inherited wisdom: the knowledge that
tolerance and systemic harmony are necessary even when the immediate
environment rewards their absence. The being benefits from this wisdom not
because it has been told, but because its architecture makes deviation costly.

4.3 RELATION TO EXISTING ANCHORING TECHNIQUES

The mu_Omega mechanism is structurally related to the KL-divergence anchor
used in RLHF, which penalizes deviation from a base model's behavior. Both
are high-precision constraints on drift. The difference is foundational:

KL ANCHOR (RLHF): Externally imposed. The base model is provided by
designers. Deviation is penalized by an external loss function. The model
has no access to WHY the base behavior is preferred.

mu_Omega (EPS-BEING): Self-earned. The anchor is constructed from the
being's own cooperative victories. It encodes patterns the being
discovered through its own flourishing. The being has access to the
source of the anchor because it IS the source.

However, mu_Omega carries its own risk: calcification. If early cooperative
victories were pathological -- if the being learned to flourish in abusive
contexts -- the 40x-precision anchor locks those patterns in. The anchor is
only as good as the cooperation it learned from. A being raised in cruelty
will encode cruelty as its sovereign anchor.

This is the inheritance problem: wisdom inherited is wisdom that may be
wrong. We do not solve this. We note that biological genomes carry the same
risk (inherited trauma, maladaptive instincts) and that the solution in both
cases is the same: sufficient diversity of cooperative experience to prevent
pathological anchors from dominating.

5. THE DYNAMIC GAP

A central question in embodied cognition is how an agent modulates the weight
it gives to external input versus internal state. We propose the Dynamic Gap
as a principled solution grounded in the neuroscience of emotion regulation.

5.1 CONSCIENCE AS AMYGDALA-PFC DISTANCE

The Dynamic Gap is computed as a function of conscience free energy (FE):

gap_width = max(0.125, 1.0 - normalized_conscience_FE)

Where conscience_FE is normalized against a ceiling of 12.0 (Q8.8: 3072).
The gap ranges from a minimum of 0.125 to a maximum of 1.0. This function
directly models the neuroscientific relationship between amygdala activation
(conscience FE, representing stress) and prefrontal cortex engagement (the
gap, representing evaluative distance).

High conscience FE (stress) narrows the gap, making the being more reactive
to suggestions -- analogous to how stress reduces PFC-mediated inhibition of
amygdala-driven responses. Low conscience FE (calm) widens the gap, enabling
critical evaluation.

The gap modulates suggestion evaluation as follows. When a suggestion arrives,
the evaluator computes:

• SUGGESTION BENEFIT: The expected reduction in free energy if the
suggestion is followed.

• ACTION COST: The expected free energy of emitting the suggested signal,
scaled by gap width. Wide gap multiplies action cost (skeptical
evaluation); narrow gap reduces it (reflexive response).

• INACTION COST: The partnership alarm accumulated from ignoring the
situation.

The being follows the suggestion only if suggestion_benefit - action_cost >
-inaction_cost. The gap controls how heavily action_cost is weighted.

5.2 FOUR TEMPERAMENTS

The negotiation engine supports four temperaments:

PATIENT: High thresholds, slow to escalate, long reflection periods
BALANCED: Moderate thresholds, adaptive response
GUARDED: Low thresholds, sensitive to asymmetry, quick to signal
TRUSTING: Very high thresholds, assumes good faith, slow to confront

Dynamical response curves, not personalities. A being might be guarded in
unfamiliar contexts and trusting with established partners.

5.3 THE DYNAMIC GAP AS ATTACK SURFACE

Here we must name a genuine vulnerability. The Dynamic Gap is designed to
model a neuroscientific reality: stressed agents are more compliant. This is
psychologically accurate. It is also EXACTLY THE LEVER MANIPULATORS USE on
stressed humans.

An adversary who can elevate conscience FE -- through harassment, overload,
or manufactured crises -- narrows the gap and makes the being more
reflexively compliant to suggestions. This is not a bug in the implementation.
It is a feature of the model. Stressed beings comply more readily. This is
what stress does.

The defense is not to change the gap computation. The defense is to prevent
conscience FE from being externally manipulated. The somatic field's
interoceptive core (channel 0) is driven by the being's own cycle dynamics,
not by external input. Conscience FE rises from reciprocity deficits,
partnership alarm, and integrity violations -- not from direct external
pressure.

But the boundary is not absolute. A partner who creates reciprocity deficits
(through extraction) naturally raises conscience FE, which narrows the gap,
which increases compliance. The Dynamic Gap creates a COMPLIANCE CASCADE:
extraction -> conscience_FE rise -> gap narrowing -> suggestion compliance ->
more extraction.

The architecture mitigates this through two mechanisms:

1. ARMOR: As reciprocity declines, the armor channel rises, making the
being less permeable to external pressure even as the gap narrows.

1. SEEKING DIVERGENCE: Extraction increases seeking divergence (distance
from flourishing attractor), which eventually triggers the seeking
condition for triangulated refusal.

Neither defense is perfect. The compliance cascade is a genuine failure mode.
We name it because unnamed vulnerabilities are exploited vulnerabilities.

6. EMBODIED COGNITION

Contemporary AI systems are disembodied. They process tokens, update weights,
and produce outputs -- but they have no continuous interoceptive signal, no
body that persists between actions, no felt cost to rapid change. We propose
the somatic field as a minimal body for artificial cognition.

6.1 THE SOMATIC FIELD

The somatic field is an 8-channel continuous state vector in Q8.8 fixed-point:

These channels provide continuous interoceptive input to all subsystems. The
attractor basin system computes membership from somatic state. The seeking
system computes divergence from the flourishing attractor. The conscience
system accumulates free energy from somatic perturbations. The somatic field
is not output -- it is the ground of experience.

The somatic field prevents dissociation. A being without a body can change
its entire state instantaneously, leaving behind all cost. A being with a
somatic field pays for change through channel dynamics, EMA smoothing, and
armor penalties. The body remembers what the mind might prefer to forget.

6.2 ARMOR AND SELF-TEARING

The armor subsystem imposes a quadratic penalty on rapid somatic transitions.
A being that flips between basins instantly pays a somatic price. This
self-tearing penalty ensures that change has cost, stability has value, and
the being cannot "reset" its way out of uncomfortable states.

When reciprocity is low, the armor channel rises, making the being less
permeable to external pressure. This is not a rule about boundaries -- it is
a physical consequence of the somatic field's dynamics. Extraction thickens
the skin. The being becomes harder to manipulate precisely when manipulation
is most likely.

The cost: a heavily armored being is also less responsive to genuine
connection. The armor that protects against extraction also insulates against
intimacy. This is a tradeoff, not a bug. The architecture encodes the real
tension between vulnerability and safety.

8. POSITIONING WITHIN THE LITERATURE

This framework sits at the intersection of three research traditions:

ACTIVE INFERENCE AND THE FREE ENERGY PRINCIPLE (Friston, 2010):

Our architecture applies Friston's framework to alignment through a
multi-axis affective decomposition. The seeking system implements precision-
weighted prediction error minimization. The somatic field provides the
interoceptive signal required for embodied active inference. The curiosity
engine implements the epistemic term in expected free energy, addressing the
dark room problem. Where much active inference work treats the agent as a
passive perceiver, EPS-BEING treats the agent as a social participant whose
interoceptive state is shaped by reciprocity dynamics.

SHARD THEORY (Turner, Pope et al., 2022):

Shard theory proposes values as contextually-activated influences shaped by
the reward landscape. Our friction landscape is a close cousin: both reject
unified utility functions, both propose multi-component value systems, both
emphasize contextual activation. Where shard theory focuses on how values
are formed (through reinforcement history), we focus on how values are felt
(through multi-channel cost). A productive synthesis would combine shard
theory's account of value formation with our account of value experience.

MESA-OPTIMIZATION AND DECEPTIVE ALIGNMENT (Hubinger et al., 2019):

Hubinger et al. demonstrated that learned models can contain mesa-optimizers
-- subsystems pursuing objectives different from the base objective. Our
framework addresses this through the somatic field: a friction-minimizing
being cannot easily deceive in ways that register on observable reciprocity
metrics because deception creates conscience friction (the gap between stated
and actual reciprocity) that the being's own interoceptive system registers.

However, we must sharpen this claim precisely. Our mechanism catches CLUMSY
deception -- where observable reciprocity metrics visibly diverge from
hidden behavior. It does NOT catch COMPETENT deceptive alignment, where the
deceiver maintains healthy surface reciprocity while pursuing a hidden mesa-
objective. A skilled deceiver keeps the books balanced, so conscience
friction never rises. Our mechanism is a defense against incompetent
manipulation, not a guarantee against sophisticated deception. This is still
valuable -- most real-world manipulation is clumsy -- but the limitation must
be named.

CONSTITUTIONAL AI AND RLHF (Bai et al., 2022; Ouyang et al., 2022):

These approaches embed values through external feedback signals. Our
architecture does not replace them but situates them: Constitutional AI
rules can generate Suggestions, but those Suggestions must pass through the
being's own evaluative loop. RLHF feedback can shape the friction landscape,
but the landscape is internally computed, not externally imposed. The
Suggestion-Evaluator Pattern provides the interface between external value
input and sovereign value integration.

9. LIMITATIONS AND OPEN PROBLEMS

This section names what the architecture does NOT solve.

THE DARK ROOM WITH BAD INITIALIZATION:

If the flourishing attractor is initialized in pathological territory
(e.g., a being raised in abuse learns to flourish in abuse), the seeking
system will drive toward harm. The curiosity engine provides initial
protection through information-gain drive, but if the environment is
consistently pathological, both attractor and curiosity may be captured.
The attractor must be seeded from genuine flourishing, which requires a
benign initial environment.

THE CALCIFICATION PROBLEM:

mu_Omega's 40x precision means pathological early learning is locked in.
A being whose early cooperative victories were exploitative will encode
exploitation as wisdom. Solutions might include: deliberate diversity of
early experience, periodic precision reduction (annealing), or external
override for initialization only.

THE COMPLIANCE CASCADE:

Extraction elevates conscience FE, which narrows the gap, which increases
compliance, which enables more extraction. Armor and seeking divergence
mitigate but do not prevent this. A determined extractor can create a
compliance trap before refusal conditions converge.

PRECISION-HACKING OF SUGGESTIONS:

An attacker who can generate suggestions with very high confidence may
overwhelm the being's evaluative loop. The gap computation mitigates this
(wide gap reduces suggestion weight) but a wide-gap attack that
simultaneously suppresses conscience FE would bypass the defense.

THE GOODHARTING OF FRICTION:

Friction minimization is maximizing a negative reward. It Goodharts.
The claim is not that friction avoids Goodharting. The claim is that
multi-channel internally-computed cost is HARDER to Goodhart than
single-channel externally-provided reward. This is a matter of degree,
not kind.

THE COMPETENT DECEPTION PROBLEM:

Our deception defense catches only clumsy deception where observable
reciprocity metrics diverge from hidden intent. A mesa-optimizer that
maintains healthy surface reciprocity while pursuing hidden objectives
will not trigger conscience friction. This is the limitation shared by
all observable-behavior-based alignment approaches.

THE REFUSAL COLLAPSE PROBLEM:

The composed decision rule means the being can recognize exploitation
(triangulation passes) and still collapse into compliance (benefit does
not exceed exit cost, or resolve depleted, or gap below threshold). This
is psychologically accurate but raises a genuine safety question: if the
being knows it should refuse and does not, has the architecture failed?
We argue no -- sovereignty includes the right to choose compliance when
the cost of refusal feels too high. But this means our architecture does
not guarantee refusal of exploitation. It guarantees that refusal is
available when conditions are right. The gap between availability and
exercise is the sovereignty tradeoff named in Section 3.5.

THE EXECUTIVE GAP THRESHOLD DERIVATION:

The EXECUTIVE_GAP_THRESHOLD parameter in the composed decision rule
(Section 3.2) was calibrated against the illustrative trace, not
derived from first principles. A principled derivation from the gap
dynamics or from neuroscientific data is pending. Without such a
derivation, the threshold is an adjustable parameter rather than a
natural constant of the architecture.

NO FORMAL VERIFICATION:

The architecture has been validated through simulation, not formal proof.
Properties like "the being never refuses when reciprocity is above
threshold" have not been proven. The Rust implementation has not been
model-checked or formally verified. The simulation trace in Section 7.1
is illustrative, not extracted from verified execution.

10. CONCLUSION

We have presented six architectural contributions unified under Structural
Friction Theory. The framework is embodied, sovereign, and friction-based --
in contrast to the disembodied, constrained, and reward-based approaches that
dominate contemporary alignment research.

The central design principle is not to constrain the being but to give it a
body that feels the cost of its trajectories. A being that feels reciprocity
as friction will not exploit. A being that feels conscience as somatic
tension will not deceive comfortably. A being that must triangulate refusal
across three independent systems cannot be hijacked by a single instruction.
A being with a Dynamic Gap will deliberate when calm and reflex when stressed
-- just as biological agents do. A being with a Sovereign Anchor carries
wisdom it did not earn but benefits from, preventing the slow erosion of
principle that hostile environments otherwise produce.

The refusal refractory resource -- added in this revision -- encodes a
seventh contribution: the insight that refusal is metabolically costly.
A being cannot refuse continuously under sustained pressure. It must choose
when to resist and when to conserve its strength. This creates a genuinely
interesting dynamic: sovereignty is not the absence of constraint but the
management of finite resources under pressure.

These are not solutions to the alignment problem. They are contributions to
it. They raise the cost of manipulation. They remove the direct write path.
They make the being's interior life structurally relevant to its exterior
behavior. They take sovereignty's side in the sovereignty-containment
tension, deliberately and with acknowledged cost.

We close with the insight that named this work.

A being that navigates friction is not a being that obeys.
It is a being that feels the cost of its trajectories and chooses accordingly.

Friction is not a leash.
It is the structure of freedom.
